03
Welcome

Your journey into the world of theatre begins here.

Something remarkable happens when a young person steps into a rehearsal room for the first time. They arrive unsure. They leave taller. At CentreStage Performing Arts Academy, that transformation is the whole point — and we have watched it happen more than 350 times a year.

CPAA is now Geelong's largest theatre education program, and we have built it on one stubborn belief: talent is everywhere, and opportunity should be too. You do not need experience. You do not need to audition. You do not need to already be good at this. You need to want it — and we will take care of the rest.

Every single student performs in a real production, on a real stage, in front of a real audience. Not a select few. Everyone.

As part of CPAA in 2027, you will

  • Learn and grow through tailored training and workshops designed and led by VIT registered teachers and industry professionals.
  • Build your performance portfolio with experiences that strengthen your stage and creative skills.
  • Take part in a full-scale musical and gain genuine insight into the industry.
  • Receive membership of CentreStage Agency, included in your fees for the first time in 2027.
  • Form lasting friendships with performers who share your passion.

At CentreStage we don't just teach theatre — we create a community where young people can discover who they are, what they love, and how far their creativity can take them. Welcome aboard. Your future in theatre starts now.

05
The program

About the Academy.

CentreStage has long recognised the need for an educational platform tailored to the unique demands of both the Geelong and Australian theatre markets. Our mission is to deliver exceptional and measurable learning outcomes, developed by some of Geelong's most esteemed educators and performers.

The Academy meets the needs of young people aged 4 to 18, offering a programme that not only enhances performance skills but also instils theatre etiquette and practical skills such as costume and make-up, and provides opportunities to collaborate with leading theatre professionals.

This four-term tuition programme aligns with the school calendar, ensuring consistency and balance for students. Participants also enjoy attending large-scale musicals produced by CentreStage.

Your year at a glance

Term one

Skill development workshops in practical and stage craft with guest speakers, audition preparation, and attending Frozen plus a Q&A with the cast at the Geelong Arts Centre.

Term two

Workshops in vocal technique, dance and stage craft. Casting and early rehearsals as students begin working towards their full-scale musical.

Term three

Singing, acting and dance rehearsals, building the musical scene by scene and preparing showcase material for the end of year performance.

Term four

Full-scale musical performances across October and November, further workshops, audition preparation, and our showcase at Costa Hall.

Classes operate on Saturdays during the Victorian school terms.

06
The outcomes

What you'll see in your young one.

Parents rarely tell us about the singing. They tell us their child started putting their hand up in class. Ordered their own meal. Made a speech at a family birthday. Musical theatre trains the whole person, and the change usually shows up at home long before it shows up on stage.

Confidence

Performing asks for a small, supported risk every single week. Each one survived makes the next one easier.

Communication

Projecting, articulating, holding a room's attention and reading it — skills that transfer straight into classrooms and interviews.

Collaboration

A show only works if everyone shows up prepared. Students learn accountability to a group, not just to themselves.

Resilience

Not every audition ends in the hoped-for role, and things go wrong on stage. Students learn to recover and keep going.

Empathy

Playing someone else's life is practice at understanding it. Character work builds genuine perspective-taking.

Focus & discipline

Learning lines, holding choreography and taking direction builds attention and follow-through that schools notice.

What the research shows

BehaviourA large randomised study across 42 schools and 10,548 students found expanded arts education reduced disciplinary infractions by 3.6 percentage points.
WritingThe same study measured a 13% of a standard deviation improvement in writing achievement.
CompassionStudents showed an 8% of a standard deviation increase in compassion for others, alongside higher school engagement and college aspirations.
LiteracyA meta-analysis of 80 studies found classroom drama improves story recall and understanding, reading achievement, oral language and writing.

Sources: Bowen & Kisida, Houston Arts Access Initiative randomised controlled trial; Podlozny (2000) meta-analysis of classroom drama research. External research on arts education generally, not CentreStage-specific data.

Our standards

Held to the same standard as a school.

We are a theatre company, but we are an education provider first — and we hold ourselves to educational standards, not hobby-club ones.

Multi-award-winning

Geelong Chamber of Commerce Business Excellence Awards

Business Excellence Hall of Fame inductee, and winner of Small Business of the Year, the Corporate Social Responsibility Award, Youth Entrepreneur of the Year, and twice the Health & Wellbeing Promoting Workplace Award. Rare recognition for an arts organisation, and a reflection of how the Academy is run.

Department of Education guidelines

Our teaching practice, planning and child safety procedures follow Victorian Department of Education guidance, delivered by a team led by VIT registered teachers and industry professionals.

SWPBS teaching style

We teach using School-Wide Positive Behaviour Support — the same framework used across Victorian schools. Expectations are taught explicitly, positive behaviour is recognised, and students are supported consistently by every teacher in every room.

Inclusive development

Every student is met where they are. Adjustments for neurodiverse students, students with disability and EAL learners are ordinary practice, not special cases.

Digital child-safe platforms

All communication runs through Studio Pro and Seesaw — monitored, parent-visible platforms. No private messaging between staff and students, ever.

Why it matters to you

It means the person teaching your child is qualified and accountable, the way they are managed in the room is deliberate and consistent, and every message about your child passes through a platform you can see. Excellence on stage starts with rigour off it.

The 2027 season

Three musicals.
One extraordinary year.

Not a class recital. Not a school assembly. Three fully-staged musicals with real sets, real costumes, a live audience and a curtain call your family will never stop talking about.

Annie Kids*Juniors · 4–7

Leapin' lizards! One of the best-loved musicals of all time, reimagined as a bright thirty-minute adventure for our youngest performers. Annie is a plucky orphan determined to find her family, escaping the wicked Miss Hannigan and winning the heart of Oliver Warbucks. With Tomorrow, It's the Hard-Knock Life and Easy Street, it is a story about optimism and the family you choose.

Trolls The Musical Jr*Middles · 8–12

Based on the DreamWorks film, this joyously colourful musical follows the relentlessly optimistic Poppy and the grumbling Branch as they journey beyond their village to rescue their friends. Packed with pop anthems including Can't Stop the Feeling!, it celebrates friendship, difference and finding your own kind of happiness — with featured moments for every performer.

Spamalot*Seniors · 12–18

Lovingly ripped off from Monty Python and the Holy Grail, this riotous send-up follows King Arthur and his Knights of the Round Table on their absurd quest — via killer rabbits, taunting Frenchmen and a very demanding Lady of the Lake. The Young@Part edition keeps every ounce of the anarchic wit, crowned by Always Look on the Bright Side of Life.

* Titles are preliminarily approved and held for CentreStage, and remain subject to change. If a professional production announces an Australian tour, these titles may be updated in line with our licensing arrangements.

12
Our classes

Find the right class.

One morning a week. No audition, no experience needed, no one left in the wings. In 2027 the Academy focuses entirely on our Saturday troupes, so every ounce of our teaching strength goes into your child and the show they are building.

Junior Troupe

Ages 4–7

A two-hour Saturday morning class with multiple breaks. Students learn stage space, improvisation, theatre etiquette, singing, dancing and acting, and build friendships that last a lifetime. In 2027 the Junior Troupe works towards Annie Kids, plus the Be Our Guest showcase.

Middle Troupe

Ages 8–12 · grades 2–6

A three-hour Saturday rehearsal with a choice of four class times. Students develop stage craft, improvisation, theatre etiquette, singing, dancing and acting while working towards a major musical. In 2027 the Middle Troupes stage Trolls The Musical Jr.

Senior Troupe

Ages 12–18 · grades 7–12

A three-hour Saturday rehearsal with a choice of class times. Students refine performance technique across singing, dancing and acting, and take on more demanding material and responsibility. In 2027 the Senior Troupes stage Spamalot.

Not offered in 2027

Dance classes, private singing lessons and private vocal and piano lessons are not part of the 2027 program. Talent Agency private coaching continues separately — visit centrestageagency.org.au.

Child safety

The safety of every child comes first.

At CentreStage, the safety and wellbeing of all children and young people in our care is our highest priority. We have zero tolerance for child abuse in all its forms.

We are fully committed to creating and maintaining a child-safe environment that promotes respect, inclusion and empowerment. This commitment aligns with the Victorian Child Safe Standards and relevant legislation, including the Child Wellbeing and Safety Act 2005 (Vic) and the Children, Youth and Families Act 2005 (Vic). We recognise our moral and legal obligations to implement practices that protect children from harm, abuse and exploitation.

Our approach

  • Child Safe Standards — we comply with the Victorian Child Safe Standards and regularly review our policies and practices to ensure they remain effective and up to date.
  • Code of Conduct — all staff, volunteers and contractors are bound by our Child Safety Code of Conduct, which sets clear expectations for behaviour with children.
  • Qualified, registered leadership — our program is designed and led by VIT registered teachers and industry professionals, held to the professional standards of the Victorian Institute of Teaching.
  • Screening — we conduct thorough recruitment processes, including Working with Children Checks and National Police Checks, for everyone who works with children.
  • Reporting and responding — robust procedures for managing concerns and allegations. All concerns are taken seriously and acted on in line with Victorian law and the Reportable Conduct Scheme.
  • Training and awareness — staff and volunteers receive regular training on child safety, mandatory reporting and recognising the signs of abuse.
  • Cultural safety and inclusion — a culturally safe environment for all children, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, children from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds, and children with disability.
17
Child safety

How we put it into practice.

Empowering children

We actively involve children in decisions that affect them and ensure they have access to information about their rights and the avenues available to raise concerns. By fostering open communication, we empower children to speak up if they ever feel unsafe or uncomfortable.

Continuous improvement

CentreStage is committed to continuously improving our child safety practices. We engage with families, children and the community to ensure our approach reflects the highest standards of care. Regular audits and reviews maintain compliance with Victorian law and adapt to new best practice.

Volunteers and families

Every family member who volunteers holds a current Working with Children Check, is inducted into our Code of Conduct, and is identifiable on site. Volunteers are always supervised and never placed in unaccountable one-to-one situations with children.

Read our policies and procedures

Every CentreStage policy is published in full — child safety, code of conduct, reporting, wellbeing, inclusion and more. Scan the code to read them at centrestage.org.au/health, or contact our Child Safety Officer at cpaa@centrestage.org.au with any question or concern.
